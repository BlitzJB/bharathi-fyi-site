# Research: Creator writing frameworks with verifiable results

> Compiled 2026-09-06. The teachable mechanics of top LinkedIn creators, with credibility evidence.

## Justin Welsh (575k+ followers, ~$10M solo business, 900M+ impressions)

**3-Part Post Anatomy — written in this order (body first, hook second):**
1. **Meat** — the core teaching, written first.
2. **Trailer** — everything before "…more". Three lines: (1) short scroll-breaking opener, (2) context/tension builder, (3) the hook line promising a payoff tied to a **specific result**.
3. **Summary + CTC** — TL;DR recap, then a **Call-to-Conversation** (question / contrarian prompt / offer to answer in comments), not a call-to-action.

**Hook sequence:** Relatable Enemy → Hero → Teaser.

**Content Matrix (ideation):** pick outcome (engagement / thought leadership / subscribers / sales) → list niche **themes** → cross with his **8 styles**: Observational, Actionable, Motivational, Analytical, Contrarian, X vs Y, Present vs Future, Listicle. Every theme × style = a post idea.

**PAIPS formula:** Problem → Agitate → Intrigue → Positive Future → Solution. (One post: 276,798 impressions, 600+ subscribers.)

**Production:** batch 20 posts every Saturday in 1 hour (Curate → Templatize → Rapidly create → Distribute). No external links at publish; edit the link in after the initial scoring window. Write about what the audience literally said, in their words.

## Ship 30 for 30 — Dickie Bush & Nicolas Cole (Cole: 100M+ views, 7-figure ghostwriting agency sold)

**Endless Idea Generator:** Specific topic × Credibility type × 4A angle × Proven format.
- Niche down until combinations appear ("Money → Investing → in your 20s → to buy rental property in your 30s").
- Credibility stance: "I'm the expert" / "I'm curating experts" / "personal experience".
- **4A angles:** Actionable ("here's how"), Analytical ("here are the numbers"), Aspirational ("yes, you can"), Anthropological ("here's why").
- Formats: How-To, Lessons, Mistakes, Examples, Reasons…

**2-Year Test:** write to you-minus-two-years. "To you, everything about your life is 'normal'" — the obvious-to-you is content.

**Headline mechanics — 5 pieces:** How Many + WHAT + WHO + FEEL + Outcome. Governing rule: **"Clear > Clever. If the reader is confused, their default answer is 'No.'"**

**13 headline templates:** BIG numbers; Dollar signs ("even $0 works — it's the specificity"); Credible names; "This Just Happened"; Question w/ unconventional answer; Success Story ("How I Went From X To Y Without Z"); Things That Shouldn't Go Together; For The Industry; Topic-Within-Topic; X-Number listicle; Guess The Future; Advice Inverted; Unexpected Opinions.

**Formatting laws:** "Skimmability is Readability." **1/3/1 rhythm** (1 punchy line → 3-line paragraph → 1 closer; 1/5/1 variant). 1 line = 1 idea; any 3+ items become bullets; first sentence of every block is a mini-headline. **Rate of Revelation:** every sentence pushes forward; cut restatements.

**LinkedIn structure:** Lead-In → Main Points → CTA. Hook = first ~5 lines, only job is the "See more" click. Body: "one sentence, 3 bullets, repeat." **Golden Intersection:** actionable + aspirational (personal story + practical takeaway) outperforms.

## Lara Acosta (0 → ~400k followers since 2021)

**SLAY:** **S**top the scroll (result/contradiction/number first) → **L**esson (one concept per paragraph, "each line earns its place") → **A**ctionable, anchored to reality ("Lost my biggest client in month 4" beats abstractions) → **Y**ou (reader-addressed close, exactly **one** CTA — "three options means most pick none").

Mechanics: first line 8–10 words (mobile cutoff); sentences ≤8–10 words; posts 150–250 words; arc = transformation → struggle → lesson → resolution. Mix: 80% tactical-educational / 20% personal. Positioning: "here's what I *did* — and what happened," not "what I learned."

## Matt Barker (~154k followers, 50M+ impressions/2yrs, ~$1M content revenue)

**Layered hooks** — add one layer at a time: core idea + timeline + number + dream outcome + curiosity words. ("I work fast on LinkedIn" → "…7 posts in 20 minutes that hit 100K views without writer's block.")

Top templates (60–70% of his viral hooks): "How to [outcome] without [obstacle]" / "The #1 best way to [outcome]" / "How to [action] in under [time]".

Post mechanics: hook → one-line context → numbered/arrow list of 3–10 one-line items → **P.S. question**. ~131 words average. Keeps a running pain-point list so topic + structure are never missing.

## Jasmin Alić (0 → 340k+, #1 by Favikon 2023 / Taplio 2024 / Aware 2025; clients: Microsoft, Shell)

- **"The hook is 90% of a good post."** Prefer 1-line hooks.
- **Last-line-first trick:** take the draft's last line — usually the power statement — and promote it to the hook.
- **Re-Hook:** the line after the hook must "slam the door behind" the reader.
- **"Dear Son" rule:** write to exactly one reader, intimately.
- End every post with a **P.S.** (lowers reply barrier).
- **Pinned-comment strategy:** add 3–7 of his own comments (~10 min after posting) with questions/bonuses/photos — boosts time-on-post. Never comment "Great post"; comment for everyone reading, not just the author; engage 20–30 same-niche + 10 adjacent creators.

## Katelyn Bourgoin (4x founder, 200k+ followers, Why We Buy ~70k subs)

**Trigger Technique:** interview recent happy buyers → extract trigger event, goals, alternatives tried, pains, selfish desires, "primary job to be done" → target **moments** (milestone birthday, new job, prepping a presentation), not demographics. For posts: aim at readers *in* a trigger moment.

## Wes Kao (Maven/altMBA co-founder)

- **Sales, Then Logistics:** open with stakes/why-now, never mechanics.
- **MOO — Most Obvious Objection:** name and pre-empt it in the text before publishing.
- **Signposting:** connective words ("because", "for example", "first/second") over heavy formatting.
- **Concision ≠ short:** "economy of words and density of insight"; the bottleneck is unclear thinking.
- **State changes:** vary experience (story → data → question → example) to hold attention.

## Cross-cutting hook science

- **Curiosity gap** (Loewenstein 1994): fires on a specific known-unknown gap, sized right, with enough context to care. Upworthy industrialized it until novelty collapsed — **any hook pattern decays with saturation**.
- **Negativity bias, quantified** (Nature Human Behaviour 2023; ~105k Upworthy headline RCT variants, 370M impressions): **each additional negative word raised CTR ~2.3%; positive words decreased clicks; sadness > anger/fear.**
- **Concreteness caveat:** vague curiosity-gap hooks backfire; concreteness increases selection (Nature Sci Rep 2024).
- **Broetry** (one-sentence-per-line, Josh Fechter 2017): now reads as bait. Modern consensus: mixed rhythm — short lines plus real paragraphs, substance first.

## Consensus mechanical rules (synthesis)

- One idea per post; one CTA per post.
- First line ≤8–10 words; first 2–3 lines earn the "See more".
- 1/3/1 or 1/5/1 rhythm; 1–2 sentence paragraphs; bullets for 3+ items; re-hook after the hook.
- Specificity everywhere: numbers, dollar amounts, timeframes, named tools.
- ~130–250 words for text posts; TL;DR near the end; close with question/P.S. (question CTAs ≈ 2–3x more comments than link CTAs); reply to every comment in hour one.
- Links: cost reach in body; Welsh edits links in later; 2026 algorithm also penalizes obvious "bridge" posts funneling to a comment link — prefer no-link value posts with profile-level funnels.

## Voice capture (ghostwriting agency processes)

- 2–4 weeks of extraction: recorded 45–60 min interviews + analysis of existing communication → a **"voice bible"**: 20–30 verbatim quote sentences, 10–15 signature phrases/tics, an explicit **never-say list**, tone/emotional-range description.
- **Scene-based prompts**, not reflective questions: "Take me to the day you decided to start. Where were you?" Chase sensory detail. Avoid yes/no, leading, and "what did you learn?" questions (they trigger analytical mode).
- Best recurring question: **"What did you tell a client/colleague/investor last week that made them lean forward?"**
- Listen for: sentence length/rhythm, fragment frequency, jargon level, repeated intensifiers, native metaphor domains, humor style, emotional register.
- Calibrate: write 5–10 samples, ask "Does this sound like you? Words you'd never use?"; offer style variants if the client can't articulate.

## Copywriting fundamentals creators explicitly import

- **Ogilvy:** "Five times as many people read the headline… you have spent eighty cents out of your dollar" → mapped to the hook.
- **Schwartz's 5 awareness stages:** match the opening to the reader's awareness; top-of-funnel LinkedIn = problem-aware, not product-aware.
- **Halbert:** "a hungry market, not a brilliant burger" — offer/audience beats prose; echoes Bourgoin's trigger moments.

## Anti-patterns (creators + platform now punish)

- AI-slop tells: em-dash overuse, "It's not X, it's Y", stacked adverbs, forced tricolons, "thrilled/humbled to announce", rocket/fire emoji clusters. ~54% of long-form LinkedIn posts estimated AI-written (2025); LinkedIn shipped a **"Seems like AI slop" report button (July 2026)** — flagged content loses distribution beyond immediate network.
- Engagement bait: "comment YES for the PDF", emoji polls, rage-bait, tag-storms — down-ranked; also "bridge" posts.
- Pure broetry; generic advice posts (no numbers, no named tools, no personal stake); multi-CTA endings; link-in-post at publish; "Great post" comments.
