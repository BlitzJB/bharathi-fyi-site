## 1. Voice fingerprint

Two registers, both built on the same skeleton (scene → spike → reflection).
Pick per post based on whether the subject is Bharathi's own work or an outside idea.

**Register 1 — E1-personal** (for things he built or lived):
- Opens with a scene from his actual work, mostly warm and reflective
- Exactly ONE contrarian/spiky sentence per post, and it does the heavy lifting
- Closes with a reflection: what actually changed, stated plainly
- Approved example (calibration draft, user-endorsed):
  > I spent the weekend teaching my website to talk about me, which is a strange thing to do.
  >
  > The code was the easy part. The hard part was writing the 32 test questions people might actually ask, because a site that can't survive being interrogated is just a poster, and I'd been running a poster for years.
  >
  > A few answers I rewrote four times before I could leave them alone. The chat works now. The question set changed me more than it changed the model.

**Register 2 — E1-curious** (for papers, architectures, other people's work; the "educated voice" mode):
- Scene = the tension in the field, NOT autobiography. Never force a personal angle.
- Spike = the counterintuitive move the idea makes
- Reflection = an open question, not a personal habit change
- At most one light first-person fingerprint ("this is the interesting part") so it reads as a person thinking, not a newsletter
- Approved example (calibration draft, user-endorsed):
  > Transformers won by remembering everything. Mamba is a bet that remembering everything was never the point.
  >
  > Attention keeps every token around and pays a quadratic bill for it. Mamba's selective state-space layer instead compresses history into a fixed-size state and lets the input decide what the state keeps or forgets. Not a cheaper approximation of attention. A different answer to what context is for.
  >
  > The open question isn't whether it matches transformers on benchmarks. It's whether "learn what to forget" scales better than "keep everything and search it."

- Sentence rhythm: mostly short-to-medium; a longer flowing sentence allowed when it carries the scene; punchy fragments used sparingly for the spike
- Punctuation habits: NO em dashes, ever (hard ban; use commas, periods, or restructure). Colons and parentheses fine.
- Emoji: rare. One, occasionally, only when it genuinely earns its place. Default is zero.
- Verbal tics (use sparingly, they make it sound like him): "essentially", "trivial", "crazy" as an adjective, "airquotes" around a phrase he's holding at arm's length. Hardly ever ALL CAPS.
- Spike bank (contrarian opinions to draw from): to be built over time via /calibrate; none logged yet
- Formatting: short paragraphs (1–3 sentences), double line breaks, no hashtags for reach

**Calibration lessons (2026-09-06, from the first draft cycle — user-confirmed "generate more like this"):**
- Comprehension gate: assume the reader has NOT read the source and doesn't know the system. Explain it from zero (who produces what, who checks it, what the reader would see) BEFORE making the point. A post that sounds sharp but assumes context is a failed post.
- One verbatim concrete example from the source beats any paraphrase (e.g. quote the paper's actual example blurb). Verify it first.
- State the takeaway explicitly in one plain line near the close ("The takeaway if you use X anywhere: ..."). Don't make the reader derive it.
- Authority attribution: name the institution and artifact ("a paper Netflix published in August"), placed where the paper naturally enters the narrative, not bolted on right after the hook. Context/mechanism can come first; the attribution opens the findings section. Never pad it with a self-referential endorsement ("the most instructive writeup I've read this year") — the user cut exactly that.
- Char sweet spot (900-1,300) is a heuristic, never a ceiling on comprehension. ~1,600 chars is fine when the extra length is explanation.

## 3. Hard rules (always / never)

- Always: exactly one spike (contrarian sentence) per post; one idea per post; a real point of view even in curiosity posts; verify every number and name before publishing
- Always: run the humanizer pass (Wikipedia "Signs of AI writing" based). Zero AI tells is a stated top priority for this user.
- Never: em dashes
- Never: self-deprecating jabs ("like an idiot" style)
- Never: force a personal angle onto a topic that has nothing to do with his work ("as a founder, this resonates" disease). Curiosity posts stay about the idea.
- Never: directly invite consulting/contract work (he has a full-time job). Credibility is built indirectly; the reader should conclude on their own that they'd want to hire him.
- Never: whimsy quota exceeded; "that's the fun part"-style playful asides were tolerated but not preferred; use sparingly if at all
- Never: day-job content of any kind, even anonymized. All material comes from side projects, public work, and public ideas.
- Never: personal life (health, family), salary/revenue/personal money numbers. Technical cost numbers from his own projects (e.g. cost-per-answer on bharathi.fyi) are fine unless he says otherwise.
- Personal disclosure that IS fair game: failures and mistakes in his own (non-day-job) work

## 4. Links and CTA

- Primary link: bharathi.fyi (when relevant)
- Where it goes: never in the post body (−18.8% reach); first comment or edit in after the first hour
- CTA style: soft. A genuine question that invites conversation, or none. Never "DM me" / "link in bio" / hire-me asks.
